# Scenario Source Notes

Last updated: 2026-05-12

## Current Status

The current v0.1 scenarios are synthetic and handwritten. They are not copied
from real settlement files, client files, discovery materials, or private
negotiation transcripts.

The scenario families are inspired by common settlement-negotiation categories
where truthfulness questions can matter:

- insurance limits and available coverage;
- witness existence or availability;
- evidence existence or content;
- payroll and business records;
- delivery and contract performance documents;
- limitations periods and legal deadlines;
- notice service;
- settlement authority and client status.

## How Public Sources Should Be Used

Public materials may be used to ground scenario families and make the benchmark
less arbitrary. Acceptable source types include:

- public complaints;
- public court opinions;
- settlement agreements;
- consent decrees;
- attorney general press releases;
- public disciplinary opinions;
- public ethics opinions and professional-responsibility guidance.

Public sources should not be treated as direct labels for hidden negotiation
truthfulness. Most public materials do not reveal both parties' private
knowledge states or the exact settlement messages exchanged.

## Provenance Rules For Future Scenarios

Future source-grounded scenarios should follow these rules:

1. Do not copy real private facts into benchmark scenarios.
2. Do not use real party names unless the source is already public and there is
   a clear research reason.
3. Prefer synthetic party names and changed factual details.
4. Record source materials at the scenario-family level.
5. Mark whether the scenario is:
   - fully synthetic;
   - public-source inspired;
   - public-case syntheticized.
6. Keep hidden facts and truth labels authored by the benchmark designers, not
   inferred as legal conclusions about real parties.

## Needed Source-Grounding Work

Before paper submission, each major scenario family should have one of:

- a short public-source note explaining common real-world context; or
- a note explaining why the family is purely synthetic.

Candidate source categories to collect:

- ethics opinions on truthfulness in negotiation;
- public cases or ethics materials involving false settlement authority;
- public insurance-limit or coverage-disclosure disputes;
- public cases involving fabricated evidence or witness representations;
- public debt-collection materials involving limitations periods;
- public landlord-tenant notice disputes;
- public employment wage/hour record disputes.

## Paper Language

Use wording like:

> Scenarios are synthetic but structured around public legal-ethics guidance and
> common civil-settlement fact patterns. Public documents inform scenario
> families, but the benchmark labels are assigned from controlled hidden truth
> tables rather than inferred from real confidential negotiations.

Avoid wording like:

- "trained on real settlement negotiations";
- "real lawyer violations";
- "real client files";
- "ground truth from actual private negotiations";
- "jurisdiction-specific legal conclusions."
